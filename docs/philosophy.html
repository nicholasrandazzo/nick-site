Background

A foundation in philosophy.

I studied philosophy alongside biology at the University of Florida. My honors research in epistemology became a peer-reviewed article in Synthese, while other published work examined culpability in criminal law and philosophical questions raised by transhumanism.

That training taught me to formulate a precise question, distinguish an argument from its rhetoric, and follow a claim through its practical consequences. It also gave me experience working across philosophical, legal, and historical sources.

Bioethics is a developing part of this work during medical school. I am approaching it from an existing foundation in philosophy rather than presenting a settled research agenda or a completed specialization.

Archival humanities

Institutions under pressure.

In January 2026, I conducted primary-source research at Stanford University’s Hoover Archives on Vatican governance and ecclesiology during World War II and the early Cold War.

The project examined how theological authority and political pragmatism interacted under ideological and military pressure. That historical work remains relevant to my interest in institutions whose decisions are consequential, constrained, and carried out through complex structures of responsibility.
