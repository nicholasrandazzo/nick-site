Biography

Toward surgery and bioethics.

Nicholas Randazzo is a medical student at Albert Einstein College of Medicine and an aspiring cardiac surgeon and bioethicist. He is interested in the disciplines required to intervene well and in how major medical treatment can alter a patient’s future.

He graduated from the University of Florida in May 2026 with a B.S. in Biology summa cum laude and a B.A. in Philosophy magna cum laude. His scientific training in the Bruner Lab included research on natural-product biosynthesis and colibactin-induced DNA damage, with work in X-ray crystallography, protein expression, and assay design.

His clinical work in ocular oncology has included ultra-widefield ophthalmic imaging and research on melanoma genetics and treatment-associated retinal disease. His philosophical work includes a peer-reviewed article in Synthese, writing on law and personhood, ongoing work in bioethics, and archival research at Stanford’s Hoover Institution.

He looks forward to his clinical vocation in cardiac surgery and to continued work in bioethics and the humanities.
